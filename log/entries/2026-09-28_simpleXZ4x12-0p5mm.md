# 2026-09-28 — simpleXZ 4x1x12 0.5 mm gate

## Question

Can shifting the 48 surface-plane partitions from 8x1x6 to 4x1x12 reduce
moving-AMR cell imbalance without sacrificing the strong runtime benefit of the
8x1x6 layout?

## Controlled change

Only the decomposition layout changed:

```text
reference: simple (8 1 6)
probe:     simple (4 1 12)
```

The base mesh remains exactly balanced at 14,520 cells/rank.

All physics and numerical settings remained frozen.

## Result

Fusion-zone geometry was preserved:

```text
W 435.187 -> 435.187 um   0.000%
D 60.9995 -> 60.9995 um  0.000%
L 685.187 -> 685.187 um  0.000%
V +0.204%
```

Load balance improved:

```text
8x1x6 max/mean imbalance = 1.683
4x1x12 max/mean imbalance = 1.401
ratio = 0.832
local min/max = 37,620 / 56,051
```

However runtime regressed:

```text
final wall/step  1.1153 -> 1.1322 s/step  (+1.5%)
final wall       2978.9 -> 3695.4 s       (+24.1%)
pressure wall    1058.5 -> 1229.3 s       (+16.1%)
thermal wall      593.4 ->  670.0 s       (+12.9%)
ClockTime        4260   -> 5672 s         (+33.1%)
```

The larger total slowdown is partly associated with a different timestep
trajectory (3264 final-interval steps rather than 2671), but even the
step-normalized cost is not better.

Beam power accounting, continuity and termination remained healthy.

## Interpretation

The experiment demonstrates an important distinction:

> Lower cell-count imbalance does not automatically mean lower wall time.

More transverse partitions reduce the peak refined-cell count per rank, but
also change processor-boundary topology and the numerical trajectory. For this
case the better balance does not compensate for those costs.

This fails the pre-declared promotion gate, which required both improved
balance and competitive actual/per-step wall cost.

## Decision

**REJECT 4x1x12 as the next campaign layout. Do not run its 1 mm confirmation.**

Keep 8x1x6 as the best demonstrated static-decomposition benchmark:
- it is much faster than the historical Scotch short baseline;
- but its imbalance grows 1.683 -> 2.625 from 0.5 to 1 mm.

The combined evidence now points away from further static-layout searching.

## Next gate

Move to checkpoint redistribution / dynamic rebalancing.

First prove a controlled workflow on a 1 mm track:
1. begin with 8x1x6;
2. stop at a normal midpoint write;
3. record pre-redistribution imbalance;
4. redistribute the decomposed dynamic mesh and fields using OpenFOAM
   `redistributePar` with a load-balancing decomposition;
5. verify the redistributed checkpoint;
6. resume to 1 mm;
7. compare W/D, continuity, post-checkpoint wall cost and final imbalance
   against the unrebalanced 8x1x6 1 mm reference.

Do not implement automatic periodic rebalancing until this checkpoint workflow
has passed.
